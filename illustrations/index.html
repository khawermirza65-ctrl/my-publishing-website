FROM AN IDEA TO AN ILLUSTRATED WORLD

A clear brief.
A story with its own character.

Your illustrations should work with the manuscript, the reader and the way the book will be printed. Start with those three things; let the visual details follow.

01 / THE STORY

Find the visual direction

Share your manuscript, the age group you’re writing for and a few references you love. We can discuss the mood, colour palette, level of detail and what the illustrations need to communicate.

02 / THE CHARACTERS

Make the cast feel consistent

Expressions, clothing and proportions need to feel like the same character from one page to the next. Establish the key designs before planning the individual scenes.

03 / THE PAGES

Think in spreads, not just pictures

Consider where the text sits, how a scene continues across a spread and which details should stay clear of the binding. Illustration and book layout work best when they are planned together.

04 / THE FINISH

Prepare for the chosen format

Agree the trim size, image resolution, colour requirements and delivery files before the final artwork is prepared. The right specifications depend on your print provider and publishing format.

BEFORE WE START

A few helpful
answers.

What should I send with my illustration enquiry?

A manuscript or story outline, the intended reader’s age, your preferred book size and a rough number of illustrations are a useful starting point. Add a few style references and tell us whether you need full-page artwork, smaller spot illustrations or both.

Can I ask for a particular illustration style?

Yes. References help describe the colours, texture and atmosphere you like. Use them to guide an original direction for your book, rather than reproducing another artist’s characters or composition.

How many illustrations will my book need?

That depends on the manuscript, page count and reading age. A picture book may use an illustration on every spread, while a chapter book may need a smaller set of spot illustrations. We can discuss the page plan before defining the scope.

How are usage rights and revisions agreed?

Ask for the permitted uses, delivery files, revision stages and any additional costs to be set out in writing before commissioning artwork. The project agreement should specify whether your illustrations can be used in print, ebooks and marketing.
