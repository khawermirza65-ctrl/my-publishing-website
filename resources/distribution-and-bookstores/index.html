THE AUTHOR’S NOTEBOOK

Available to order is not the same as on the shelf.

A practical distinction for independent authors.

← All resources

Understand what distribution provides

Distribution can make a book available for retailers or libraries to order through selected channels. An online listing, availability in an ordering system and physical shelf placement are different outcomes. Ask which of these a service actually covers.

Retailers make their own decisions

Bookshops choose stock based on their customers, buying policies and commercial terms. A distribution listing does not guarantee an order or display space. A professional cover, clear metadata and suitable trade terms may support a conversation, but they do not replace a retailer’s decision.

Check the economics

Print costs, retailer discounts, returns policies and shipping can affect the amount you receive. Review the chosen platform’s current terms before committing. Understand who controls the account, where payments go and what ongoing charges may apply.

Questions worth asking

Which formats and channels are included? Who owns the account and ISBN? Who checks the listings? What happens if files need correcting? Separate distribution preparation from paid promotion, bookstore outreach and any claim of guaranteed placement.
