Copyediting or proofreading?

Choose the right kind of attention for your manuscript.

← All resources

Start with the stage your book is at

A first draft and a typeset proof need different kinds of attention. Before booking an edit, decide whether you are still changing the structure, refining the sentences or checking finished pages. Choosing the right stage helps you avoid paying to polish passages you may later remove.

Copyediting works on the language

A copyedit focuses on grammar, clarity and consistency. It can catch changes in a character’s name, inconsistent spelling and sentences that are difficult to follow. A style sheet records decisions so they stay consistent across the manuscript. Discuss how much intervention you want before work begins.

Proofreading is the final check

Proofreading happens when the writing and layout are close to final. It looks for remaining typos, punctuation slips and visible layout inconsistencies. It is not a substitute for rewriting a confusing chapter or resolving a structural problem.

What to send an editor

Share a representative sample, your total word count, genre and publishing plans. Mention any previous editing and your preferred English spelling convention. Ask what is included, how changes will be shown and whether a final check of formatted pages is part of the scope.
