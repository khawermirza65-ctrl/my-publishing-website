Author websites and branding

A clear author website and consistent branding that fits your books.

Ask about this service

A CLOSER LOOK

An author website with a clear job to do

Clear decisions, practical deliverables and room for your input.

01

Create a home for your books

Your website should help visitors understand who you are, explore your books and find a useful next step. For a debut author, that may be a book detail page and newsletter sign-up. An established author may need a series library, media information, event details and a way for organisers to enquire.

02

Give each book its own useful page

A dedicated page can include the synopsis, formats, purchase links and approved cover image. Make the author biography specific and readable. Use reviews or endorsements only when they are genuine and you have permission to display them. Avoid filling pages with generic claims or repeated keywords.

03

Build search and usability into the structure

Descriptive page titles, logical headings, readable URLs and internal links make the site easier to understand. Mobile layout, sensible image sizes and accessible navigation matter too. SEO setup improves the foundation for discovery; it does not guarantee a ranking or replace ongoing content and outreach.

04

Know what is connected at handover

A contact form needs a working delivery route, not only a button animation. A newsletter needs a connected mailing-list service and an appropriate consent flow. Agree hosting, domain access, updates and maintenance responsibilities before launch. A preview should clearly distinguish completed features from integrations still waiting for credentials.

Questions about this service

Can you work with my current website?

Share the site URL and, when available, the source files and hosting details. We can then assess whether an update or rebuild is the practical route.

Does a website include SEO?

The brief can include technical and on-page setup. Ongoing publishing, analytics review and promotion are separate work that should be scoped explicitly.

Will I own the domain and hosting?

Keep those accounts in your name. The handover agreement should specify source files, access and any third-party subscriptions.

Who owns the domain?

It should be registered in your name.
