A CLOSER LOOK

Pictures that help a young reader follow the story

Clear decisions, practical deliverables and room for your input.

01

Start with the reader’s age and the page plan

Illustration for a picture book should support the reading experience. Share the intended age range, approved manuscript and format. A page-by-page plan helps decide where the text sits, where a scene needs space and when a page turn should reveal something new.

02

Set a consistent visual direction

Discuss the characters, setting, mood and references before final artwork begins. Character studies and an agreed style direction help keep recurring details consistent. References communicate preferences; they should not become copies of another illustrator’s protected work.

03

Approve roughs before finished colour

Sketches let you review composition, action and the space left for text. Structural changes are easier at this stage than after the artwork is finished. Agree the illustration count, whether pieces are full spreads or smaller scenes, and how revision rounds will be handled.

04

Prepare artwork for the book

The final artwork must fit the chosen trim size, bleed and layout. Print and ebook versions may need different treatment. Confirm image resolution, colour workflow and file delivery with the layout plan. Usage rights and any editable files should be described in the agreement rather than assumed.

Scope boundaries: Scope, spread count and revisions are agreed in writing before work starts.

Questions about this service

How many illustrations will my book need?

That depends on the manuscript, age range and page plan. Agree the count and types of artwork after reviewing the story.

Can you illustrate characters I already created?

Share the existing artwork and confirm you have the right to use it. We can discuss continuity and the scope needed for new scenes.

Is cover design included?

An illustrated cover and interior artwork are separate deliverables unless your written scope combines them.

Do I own the illustrations?

Usage rights should be written into your agreement.
