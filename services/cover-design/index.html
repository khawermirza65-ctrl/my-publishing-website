A CLOSER LOOK

A cover designed for the book and its readers

Clear decisions, practical deliverables and room for your input.

01

Begin with genre and audience

A memoir, a business guide and a children’s picture book should not communicate in the same visual language. A cover brief connects the subject, tone and intended reader with references you like. We consider the design at thumbnail size as well as on a physical book, so the title remains legible and the hierarchy is clear.

02

Build the whole cover, not only the front

An ebook needs a front cover image. A paperback or hardcover also needs a spine and back cover built around the printer’s template. Trim size, page count, paper choice and binding affect that template. Finalise the interior before approving the full print wrap to avoid an inaccurate spine.

03

Resolve the practical details

Supply the exact title, subtitle, author name and back-cover copy. Identify whether you need room for a barcode, a series mark or an author photograph. Discuss image licensing before choosing photographs or illustrations. The brief should state which files and usage permissions will be supplied.

04

Review concepts with a purpose

Useful feedback describes what is unclear or feels wrong for the audience. Agree the concept count and revision rounds in writing. After approval, check the title, author spelling, margins and export dimensions. A digital mockup helps visualise the book, but a physical proof is the better check for print colour and positioning.

Who it is for

Authors who want a cover that tells browsing readers what kind of book this is.

The problem it solves

Readers judge a book by its thumbnail first, and a mismatched genre signal costs attention.

What is included

  • Creative brief and genre reference review
  • Front cover concepts
  • Full wrap cover (front, spine, back) for print
  • Ebook cover file

What we need from you

  • Title, subtitle, author name and back-cover copy
  • Page count and chosen print trim size for the spine
  • Any cover examples you like or dislike

How it works

  1. Brief
  2. Concepts
  3. Refinement
  4. Final print and ebook files

Scope boundaries: Spine width depends on final page count and paper. Revision limits and file lists are set out in your written scope.

Questions about this service

Can you design a cover for a series?

A consistent type system, colour approach and series placement can help the books feel related. Share the existing titles and your plans for later volumes.

Do I need my final page count first?

A front cover concept can begin earlier. The print spine and full wrap need the final printer specifications and page count.

Will I receive editable files?

Source files, licensed assets and permitted uses should be stated in the project agreement. Confirm these requirements before design begins.

Can I supply my own artwork?

Yes, provided you hold the rights to use it.

Why do you need my page count?

The spine of a print cover can only be sized once the page count and paper are known.
